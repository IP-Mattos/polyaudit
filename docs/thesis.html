The method behind the tool

Gross numbers lie

Every public performance number — a leaderboard PnL, a backtest return, a screenshot that says +512% — is a gross figure. It is the result before the costs that are hidden from you and before the subsidies you will never receive. The only number that tells you whether you can copy it is net of both. And when that net number cannot be checked, the honest output is to refuse to give one.

true  ≈  reported − hidden costs + hidden subsidies Reconcile it against an independent figure. If the books do not close, the events are incomplete — and the number must not be published as fact.

The claim

Copyable is not the same as profitable

The question a follower actually asks is not “how much did they make.” It is “can I reproduce this with what I have.” Those are different questions, and the gap between them is exactly the two terms a gross number omits:

Hidden costs are what the reported figure already spent but did not subtract — a fee embedded inside the trade size, a spread paid on the way out, the slippage a backtest fills through as if it were free. You pay these whether or not anyone shows them to you.

Hidden subsidies are the income that arrived from being a resource rather than from being right — a volume-tier rebate, a maker reward, the exchange’s loyalty program, or simply the market rising under every position in a bull run. This income scales with capital, speed, queue position, or beta you do not have. Copy the trades without the resource and you inherit the costs but not the subsidy.

Net both terms into one figure and a familiar illusion collapses: a wallet with six figures of public profit whose trading loses money, kept alive by a rebate; a bot whose “512% backtest” is the single best run of a parameter sweep; a stock rule whose edge is half the market tide. Same arithmetic, three costumes.

The generalization

One identity, four domains

The identity is not about prediction markets. It is about any place a public number is shown without its full cost and subsidy structure. Each row below is a measurement, not an assertion — the method and data are in the repository.

DomainThe gross numberWhat it hidesWhat the net reveals
Polymarket leaderboard “+$100k profit” fee embedded in trade size; rebate reported on a separate page trading near zero; income dominated by a subsidy that scales with volume you will not have
Trading-bot backtest “+512% return” parameter optimization, look-ahead, in-sample fit, zero fees the maximum of a grid search; the same idea prints 98% to 512% depending only on the knobs — a curve fit, not an edge
Equity “it always runs” “+5.5% after the jump” market beta — the tide lifting every position about half survives as excess over the benchmark; the rest was the market, not the signal
Any “I made $X” the headline return the resource — capital, speed, queue position, tier not reproducible at your scale, because the return was rent on a resource, not a repeatable decision

Figures are drawn from this project’s own audits and pre-registered measurements. The equity and backtest rows are deliberately rounded; the exact runs, with their caveats (survivorship, event clustering, single regime), are in the research.

The feature nobody sells

Refusing is the product

A dashboard whose business is engagement will always hand you a number, because a number keeps you looking. That is precisely why its number cannot be trusted at the edges: when the underlying events are incomplete — redemptions that return empty from the API, a history truncated by size — the honest reconstruction is impossible, and the honest output is to say so.

This is the one line the incentives of a growth product forbid: NOT RELIABLE — the books do not close; do not publish these numbers. An auditor earns trust by being willing to return nothing. A funnel cannot afford to. That asymmetry is not a limitation of the method; it is the whole of it.

Reproduce it

Do not trust this page

Nothing here asks for belief. The reconciliation identity is arithmetic; the tool is a single standard-library script, or it runs entirely in your browser; every wallet this project attempted is listed with its verdict, including the refusals and the two audits it initially left out of its own table. Corrections live in the git history rather than being rewritten — two of them are retractions of the author’s own published inferences, kept because a method that cannot be caught being wrong cannot be trusted being right.

Run it on a wallet you already have an opinion about. If the number it returns disagrees with the leaderboard, the identity above tells you exactly where the gap went.

audit a wallet → the research → commission an audit →

This is a method for reading what happened, not a prediction and not financial advice. Its numbers are reconstructions from public data; where they cannot be reconciled, the correct output is to refuse.
