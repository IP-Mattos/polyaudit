The method behind the tool

Gross numbers lie

Every public performance number is a gross figure. A leaderboard PnL, a backtest return, a screenshot that says +512%. All of them report the result before the costs that are hidden from you, and before the subsidies you will never receive. If you want to know whether you can actually copy the number, you need it net of both. And when that net number cannot be checked, the honest answer is that there is no number to give.

true  ≈  reported − hidden costs + hidden subsidies Check it against an independent figure. If the books do not close, some events are missing, and the number should not be published as fact.

The claim

Copyable is not the same as profitable

A follower is not really asking how much someone made. They are asking whether they can reproduce it with what they have. Those are two different questions, and the distance between them is exactly the two things a gross number leaves out.

Hidden costs are what the reported figure already spent without subtracting. A fee buried inside the trade size. A spread paid on the way out. The slippage a backtest fills through as if it were free. You pay these whether or not anyone shows them to you.

Hidden subsidies are the income that came from being a resource, not from being right. A volume-tier rebate. A maker reward. An exchange loyalty program. Or just the market rising underneath every position during a bull run. That income scales with capital, speed, queue position, or beta you do not have. Copy the trades without the resource behind them and you keep the costs but lose the subsidy.

Put both back into the number and a familiar illusion falls apart. A wallet showing six figures of public profit whose trading actually loses money, kept alive by a rebate. A bot whose 512% backtest turns out to be the best run of a parameter sweep. A stock rule whose edge is half market tide. Same arithmetic, three costumes.

The generalization

One identity, four domains

This is not really about prediction markets. It applies anywhere a public number is shown without its full cost and subsidy structure. Each row below is a measurement rather than an opinion. The method and the data are in the repository.

DomainThe gross numberWhat it hidesWhat the net reveals
Polymarket leaderboard “+$100k profit” a fee buried in the trade size, and a rebate reported on a separate page trading near zero, and income run mostly by a subsidy that scales with volume you will not have
Trading-bot backtest “+512% return” parameter optimization, look-ahead, in-sample fit, zero fees the best run of a grid search. The same idea prints anywhere from 98% to 512% depending only on the settings. That is a curve fit, not an edge.
Equity “it always runs” “+5.5% after the jump” market beta, the tide that lifts every position roughly half of it survives once you subtract the benchmark. The rest was the market, not the signal.
Any “I made $X” the headline return the resource: capital, speed, queue position, tier not reproducible at your scale, because the return was rent on a resource rather than a repeatable decision

The figures come from this project’s own audits and pre-registered measurements. The equity and backtest rows are rounded on purpose. The exact runs, with their caveats (survivorship, event clustering, a single market regime), are written up in the research.

The feature nobody sells

Refusing is the product

A dashboard that lives on engagement will always hand you a number, because a number keeps you looking. That is the reason its number cannot be trusted at the edges. When the underlying events are incomplete, like redemptions that come back empty from the API or a history cut off by its size, an honest reconstruction is not possible, and the honest thing to do is say so.

Here is the one line a growth product cannot afford to print: NOT RELIABLE. The books do not close. Do not publish these numbers. An auditor earns trust by being willing to hand back nothing. A funnel never can. That gap is the whole point of the method.

Reproduce it

Do not trust this page

None of this asks you to believe anything. The identity is arithmetic. The tool is one standard-library script, and it also runs entirely in your browser. Every wallet this project tried is listed with its verdict, including the refusals and the two audits it first left out of its own table. Corrections stay in the git history instead of being quietly rewritten. Two of them are the author walking back his own published claims, kept there on purpose. A method you can never catch being wrong is not one you can trust when it says it is right.

Run it on a wallet you already have an opinion about. If the number it gives you disagrees with the leaderboard, the identity above shows you where the difference went.

audit a wallet → the research → deeper reads →

This is a way of reading what happened. It is not a prediction and not financial advice. The numbers are reconstructions from public data, and where they cannot be reconciled, the right output is to refuse.
