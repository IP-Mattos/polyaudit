Polymarket forensic research

Can I copy this trader?

The one question the leaderboards do not answer. Polymarket's public pnl is pre-fee, and its vol is denominated in shares, not dollars — verified on 2.3 million+ activity events across 16 audited wallets. A wallet can show six figures of public profit, lose money on every trade, and stay alive on a volume rebate you will never qualify for.

Exhibit A — The toll

The fee is never a separate line

On crypto markets the taker fee is 0.07·p·(1−p) per share, and it is embedded silently in usdcSize: a buy of 30 shares at 0.63 debits 19.39, not 18.90. The 0.49 gap is the fee. On a wallet doing hundreds of thousands of fills, that gap dwarfs the trading result. Maker fills pay zero — verified per fill on real accounts, not inferred from documentation.

On the other side of the ledger sit two separate rebate programs, both live and both officially documented: the taker rebate — automatic daily refunds by 30-day weighted-volume tier, from 3% at Bronze to 50% at Obsidian — and the maker rebate, a share of the taker-fee pool proportional to the liquidity you actually got filled. The activity feed labels them as distinct event types (TAKER_REBATE / MAKER_REBATE), and the audit counts both. Fee trackers show the toll; reward pages show the refund; nobody nets them into a single number. So a wallet paying $500k in fees while collecting $600k in rebates looks identical to one that is simply bleeding.

official  ≈  real + fees − rebates The reconciliation identity. The venue's public figure is blind to both fees and rebates, which makes it an independent check: when an audit's books do not tie out against it within 5%, events are missing and the numbers must not be published as fact.

That single netted number — trading result with the subsidy removed — is the difference between a business you can copy and one you cannot, because the rebate scales with volume you will never have. Of nine wallets audited this way early on, several of them cited in threads with hundreds of thousands of views, four were losing money on their trades and surviving on rebates.

Exhibit C — The graveyard

Strategies measured, and buried

Roughly eleven strategy families, each taken to a pre-registered test — gates written down before the data arrived, forward paper trading with controls, and adversarial re-audits of the instruments themselves. Total real-money tuition: about $12, from one hastily armed bot. Everything else died on paper. The t-statistics and sample sizes are the point: most of these looked spectacular right up until the instrument was audited.

StrategyVerdictWhat killed it
Latency arbitrage (spot feed → market book) Dead 809 opportunities × 4 delay tiers, all negative once fills were causal. The shadow's green was fill-survivorship, and every variant was systematically buying the falling side.
Late leader buying (expensive favorite, late in the candle) Dead −$25.44 over 752 paper bets. Independently reconfirmed by auditing a third-party wallet: buying the leader at 0.70–0.80 late loses pre-fee.
Intraday weather cross (temperature band dies when the observation prints) Dead 63/64 band collapses were swept in under a minute, across every timezone tested. Buried in two days, $0 staked.
Weather forecast models vs the market (GFS/ECMWF) Dead The market beat raw models in 8/9 cities (Brier 6.7 vs 9.1–11.1); 13/13 simulated tail tickets lost.
Tweet-pace prediction models Dead Market Brier 1.0 vs model 1.7; live paper trader finished −$2.95. The crowd already counts faster than the model.
Newborn-market quoting (be first in a fresh book) Dead New markets stay silent 6–18 minutes (12/12) and the first printed price is born calibrated. Early big moves are information, not price discovery to harvest.
Ladder-sum / YES+NO / monotonicity arbitrage Zero Full-ladder sums ≥ 1.056; YES+NO under $1 in 0 of 1,695 markets; monotonicity violations 0/26. No mechanical free money exists.
Favorite buying in 5-minute crypto (0.85–0.97) Dead The calibration curve said +3.2–3.9c (22k obs, t≈28) — but one-entry-per-candle shrinks the edge 5×. The only strategy that touched real money: −$12.
Momentum core (moderate strength, mid-candle) Dead Forward n>1,000 at t≈0.16–0.9 — nothing. The strongest cell was buried at t=−2.58 (−$190 simulated).
Pre-open entry (trade before the candle opens) Artifact Looked spectacular — +15.7c, t=+16.95, both halves green, 236 wallets. A regression-discontinuity check showed no jump at the exogenous candle boundary: the API timestamp is order placement, not execution.
Regime / storm detection Dead Day-to-day follow-through persistence +0.11; 20-candle gauge t=0.11; hourly persistence −0.138. The storm is only visible in the rearview mirror.
Long-horizon crypto calibration (daily/weekly/monthly) Dead The green band was survivorship: “touch” markets close early only when the touch occurs. The clean cell: +0.9c, t=0.35. One law survived: cheap crypto tails are overpriced everywhere (−3.4 to −15.9c/share, t to −48) — never buy them.
Maker one-leg deep bids Dead Adverse selection, quantified: bids at 0.70 that filled went on to hold 63.2% vs 96.4% for unfilled (−6.76c/share, t=−2.31), monotonically worse deeper (0.55 → −31 pts).
Maker two-leg pairs (small replica of the quoting machine) Dead The first simulation (+$30, 89% pairs completed) contained look-ahead — fills before the order existed. The causal redo completes 51–61% and nets −0.09/cycle (t=−13.8 to −16.2). Ask sums average 1.08; you must extract >8c per cycle just to break even.
Whipsaw harvest (buy both sides low, sell each spike) Dead Negative in every configuration; converges to zero from below (best −0.026 at 0.50/0.60). The same adverse selection in a new suit.
Hour-of-day filter Inconclusive Follow-through of 81.0% at 16:00 UTC vs <76% overnight is real — but already priced (the favorite trades 0.70–0.75), and the price test is underpowered: best block t=1.61.
Underreaction to spot moves Artifact The only cell that ever passed a pre-registered forward gate (n=64, t=+3.44, +$1.55/trade) collapsed when the same entries were re-priced at the executable ask: t=0.27, +$0.09/trade. Mean ask−print gap +17c — the “cheap leader” was a stale print, not a slow market.

Verdicts are frozen at the pre-registered gate. “Artifact” means the effect was real in the instrument and absent in the world — which is a finding about the instrument, and those turned out to be the most valuable findings of all.

Exhibit D — The method

How not to fool yourself

Four canonical falsifications shaped everything here: a collector whose attention was biased toward contested windows (+$420/day became −$0.16/window when sampled honestly); a backtest that never charged the fee and kept trading on ghost credit after going bankrupt on day three; an A/B test whose control died before the treatment's good period; and a model labeled with the same feed that generated its signals. Each one produced a rule. The rules below were all paid for.

  1. Entry prices must be executable quotes, never prints. Paid for by the stale-print autopsy: t=3.44 at the last print → t=0.27 at the ask, a +17c mean gap. Prints underreact because they are old, not because the market is slow.
  2. Audit the timestamp before the finding. The activity API's timestamp is order placement, not execution. That one field manufactured a t=16.9 “edge”.
  3. Fill simulations must be causal — no fill before its order existed. Look-ahead turned −0.09/cycle into +$30 of imaginary profit.
  4. Fee always charged, slippage measured, ruin counted. One glorious backtest ($100→$346) never paid the fee and survived its own bankruptcy on phantom credit.
  5. Watch for survivorship from early-closing markets. “Touch” markets settle early only when the touch happens; the calibration band built on them was pure survivorship.
  6. Labels must come from a channel independent of the signal feed. A fair-value model labeled by its own spot feed agreed with settlement only 62.5–73.2% of the time. Use the settlement winner or an independent index — never the feed that fired the signal.
  7. Audit the collector's attention function before believing its tape. A recorder that only kept “interesting” windows produced a +$420/day fantasy; unbiased sampling read −$0.16/window.
  8. A/B tests share period, band, and machinery — or they are not A/B tests. A control that died before the treatment's good period proves nothing about either.
  9. Do not slice small samples into significance gates. Both-halves and per-asset gates kill 44–90% of real edges at these sample sizes. Use confidence intervals and fractional sizing instead of binary gates.
  10. The strategy sample is not the calibration sample. Detecting a ~12c/trade effect takes 1,500–3,000 strategy entries, not 22,000 calibration observations.
  11. When two of your own scripts disagree, neither is valid. Not until the cause of the disagreement is found and named.
  12. Every join must print its intersection size. A silent join once dropped 24.8% of markets — exactly the ones that had flipped, which was the entire question.
  13. Instrument resolution must exceed the effect size. You cannot measure a 3c edge with a book that is 10c stale.
  14. No bot goes live without reading its own forward shadow. The one bot that touched real money (−$12) had a shadow that already said its entry window was negative. Nobody read it.

Exhibit E — For builders

API gotchas

Everything the auditor relies on, learned the slow way. All endpoints are public, keyless, and CORS-open.

  • /activity caps at ~4,000 events on a naive query. Walk backwards with &end=<oldest_ts> and dedupe on (transactionHash, asset, type, size, timestamp, side, usdcSize). A batch made entirely of one timestamp will stall a naive cursor — step the cursor back one second and only give up after two consecutive stalls.
  • Settlement truth is clob.polymarket.com/markets/{conditionId} → tokens[].winner. Short-lived closed markets are not always indexed elsewhere — capture the conditionId while the market is live.
  • gamma /markets silently ignores tag_slug. It returns unfiltered results with no error. Filter through /events instead.
  • Some wallets return zero-valued REDEEM events. Their PnL cannot be reconstructed from activity alone. Detect the case and say so — do not guess.
  • The activity timestamp is order placement time, not execution time. Any timing study built on it measures when people clicked, not when they traded.
  • Leaderboard pnl is pre-fee and vol is in shares. The two headline numbers on every profile page are not what they appear to be.
  • Be polite: space requests ≥350ms and retry on failure with backoff. A timeout must never be mistaken for the end of a history — the first version of this tool swallowed one timeout and reported four days of a three-month wallet as if it were the whole thing.

Everything on this site is a measurement of what happened, published with its sample sizes and reconciliation gaps. It is not a prediction, and none of it is financial advice. Wallet addresses shown are public on-chain data.
